RADIO COVERAGE (5RM tri-band per Miklor review; check your unit's label):
  RX : 108-136 AM (airband) | 136-174 | 200-260 | 350-390 | 400-520 MHz   (+ 65-108 broadcast FM radio)
  TX : 136-174 | 220-260 | 400-480 MHz  (US units may be locked to 144-148 / 222-225 / 420-450 MHz)
  Analog FM/AM only - it CANNOT decode P25, DMR, D-STAR, Fusion or trunked systems.

WHAT WAS DISCARDED
  * Everything in 700/800 MHz (outside the radio's range). That's the bulk of Las Vegas public safety:
      - SNACC P25 Phase II trunked: Las Vegas Fire, Clark County Fire, Henderson PD/FD, North Las Vegas
        PD/FD, Boulder City, Las Vegas City Marshals, Primm/Laughlin fire
      - LVMPD P25 Phase II trunked (fully encrypted): all LVMPD patrol, Moapa Valley FD, Laughlin, Primm
      - Nevada Shared Radio System (NHP primary, NDOT), RTC 851-853 MHz, Henderson Detention 855.4875,
        LVMPD Info 800 (851.375), 8CALL90/8TAC91-94 crossband sides, NHP/NDOT 851-853 talkarounds
  * Low band below 65 MHz (LADWP 48.180, NV Air Guard 32.350).
  * Military UHF aircraft 225-350 MHz (Nellis/Red Flag/NTTR/LAS UHF ground and tower).
  * In-range DIGITAL channels you'd only hear as noise: FBI 167.2125/167.2625/167.4375 (P25),
    Federal Protective Service 409.400, TSA LAS 406.7375, Hoover Dam PD 163.125, BLM & Lake Mead
    P25 law nets, Nellis-Creech/NNSS P25 trunk, USPS DMR, NV Energy DMR, school/airline DMR.
  * Schools, casinos, airline ground crews and other business channels (not requested).

READ BEFORE PROGRAMMING
  1. "RX ONLY" = set the channel to TX-inhibit (in CHIRP: Duplex = "off"). Transmitting on police,
     fire, federal, aviation, rail or marine frequencies is a federal crime.
  2. Ham channels need an FCC amateur license to transmit. GMRS needs a GMRS license (no test).
     The UV-5RM is not type-accepted for FRS/GMRS/MURS, so legally treat those as receive-only too.
  3. TONE is the receive tone/code as logged. For SCANNING public safety, leave RX tone OFF
     (carrier squelch) so you hear every user on the frequency; many share one channel with
     different tones. Dnnn N = DCS code (normal). Ham/GMRS repeaters need the tone set as TX tone.
  4. MODE: FM = wide (25 kHz), NFM = narrow (12.5 kHz), AM = airband. Airband channels must be in
     108-136 MHz; if a memory won't save in AM, use VFO mode for those (some firmware is VFO-only).
  5. A frequency shared by several agencies is programmed once (NOTES list every user). Ham/GMRS
     repeaters that share a frequency but use different tones/sites are kept as separate channels.
  6. Sources: RadioReference.com database (Clark Co. NV, NV DPS, NV Federal, NV Railroads,
     NV Tactical Crossband, Clark Co GMRS/ham), NOAA/NWS, NIFOG national interop plan. Community
     data goes stale - entries marked "older"/"OLD" may be inactive. Verify with a scan.
